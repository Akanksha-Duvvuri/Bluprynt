User stories — pre-consulting firm website

1. AutoCAD-style cursor
US-01 — As a visitor, I want to see an AutoCAD-style crosshair cursor replace the standard pointer across the entire site, so I immediately feel the precision and engineering identity of the firm.
Acceptance criteria:

Default browser cursor is hidden site-wide
A custom crosshair renders: two perpendicular lines extending from a small centre gap (replicating AutoCAD's crosshair, not a simple +)
The crosshair tracks the mouse in real time with no visible lag
A small coordinate readout (x, y) follows the cursor in monospace font — styled like an AutoCAD coordinate display
On mobile / touch devices, the cursor falls back gracefully (no broken overlay)
The crosshair colour matches the gold brand accent


2. Homepage — intro / hero
US-02 — As a first-time visitor, I want to land on a homepage that immediately communicates what the firm does and who is behind it, so I can decide in under 10 seconds if this is relevant to me.
Acceptance criteria:

Hero section with firm name, a one-line tagline, and a primary CTA ("View our work" / "Get in touch")
Short intro paragraph — what the firm does and who it serves
Dark charcoal background with gold typographic accents visible above the fold
Smooth scroll to sections below
No heavy load time — hero renders before any heavy assets

US-03 — As a returning visitor, I want the homepage to surface the most recent project or report so I always see something new.

3. About / founders
US-04 — As a prospect, I want a dedicated About section or page where I can read each founder's background, so I can assess their expertise and decide if I trust them.
Acceptance criteria:

One card or block per founder with: name, photo (or initials placeholder), role/title, short bio (3–5 sentences), and a link to their LinkedIn or relevant profile
Founders section feels editorial — not a generic "team grid"
Firm origin story or philosophy paragraph sits above or between the founder cards

US-05 — As a prospect, I want to see each founder's academic and professional certifications so I can verify their qualifications.
Acceptance criteria:

Certifications displayed per founder: degree(s), institution, year, professional licences (e.g. P.Eng, PMP, etc.)
Displayed as a structured list or credential badges — not buried in the bio paragraph
If a founder has a PDF credential or linked verification, there is an icon/link to it


4. Projects gallery
US-06 — As a prospect, I want to browse all projects on a single page as a grid of cards, so I can quickly scan the firm's portfolio.
Acceptance criteria:

Grid of project cards, each showing: project name, a short descriptor (sector / type), a thumbnail or placeholder image, and a visible "View project" affordance
Cards are filterable or sortable by sector (optional Phase 2)
Grid is responsive — 3 columns on desktop, 2 on tablet, 1 on mobile

US-07 — As a prospect, I want to click on any project card and be taken to that project's individual page, so I can read the full case study.
Acceptance criteria:

Each project has its own URL (e.g. /projects/project-name)
Project page includes: title, sector/type, scope summary, challenge, approach, outcome, images or diagrams
Breadcrumb or back button returns to the projects gallery
Individual project pages are shareable links


5. Services
US-08 — As a visitor, I want a clear Services page that lists exactly what the firm offers, so I can determine if my need is within scope.
Acceptance criteria:

Each service has: name, short description, typical deliverables, and who it is for
Services are visually separated — not a wall of text
A CTA at the bottom of the page points to the contact form

US-09 — As a prospect, I want each service to indicate a rough scope or engagement type (advisory, project-based, retainer, etc.) so I understand how the firm works.

6. Certifications
US-10 — As a prospect evaluating the firm, I want a dedicated credentials section so I can see all qualifications in one place without reading through full bios.
Acceptance criteria:

Degree(s) with institution and year
Professional licences and their issuing bodies
Industry certifications (software, project management, etc.)
Layout is scannable — not a paragraph dump
Section works as a standalone block embeddable on both the About page and the homepage


7. Reports / publications
US-11 — As a visitor, I want to browse published reports and whitepapers so I can assess the firm's depth of thinking before reaching out.
Acceptance criteria:

Reports listed with: title, date, short abstract, and a download or read button
PDFs open in a new tab or trigger a download — user's choice
Reports page works without authentication (open access, or with optional email gate — TBD)

US-12 — As the firm owner, I want to add a new report to the site without touching code, so I can keep publications current.
Acceptance criteria:

New report added via CMS or a simple folder + config file
No redeployment required for content-only additions (Phase 2)


8. Testimonials
US-13 — As a prospect, I want to read testimonials from past clients so I can validate the firm's reputation through third-party voices.
Acceptance criteria:

Each testimonial includes: quote, client name, title/company, and optionally a project reference
Displayed as a grid or horizontal scroll of cards — not a basic <blockquote> list
At least one testimonial visible on the homepage without needing to navigate

US-14 — As the firm owner, I want to add and reorder testimonials easily so I can feature the most relevant ones at any time.

9. Contact form + automated emails
US-15 — As a prospect, I want to fill out a contact form so I can reach the firm without needing to find an email address.
Acceptance criteria:

Fields: full name, email, company (optional), subject/service of interest (dropdown), message
Client-side validation before submit (required fields, valid email format)
Success state shown on submission — no page reload required

US-16 — As a prospect, I want to receive an automated confirmation email immediately after submitting the form, so I know my message was received.
Acceptance criteria:

Email sent within 30 seconds of form submission
Subject line: something like "We've received your enquiry — [Firm Name]"
Body: thanks, confirms what they submitted, sets expectation on response time, includes firm contact details
Email is branded — dark background, gold accent, firm name — not a generic transactional template

US-17 — As the firm owner, I want to receive an automated alert email every time someone submits the contact form, so I can follow up quickly.
Acceptance criteria:

Alert arrives within 30 seconds
Contains: submitter name, email, company, subject, full message, timestamp, and a reply-to link
Plain-text fallback if HTML email is not supported

US-18 — As a prospect, I want the automated reply to answer common questions (services, typical engagement timeline, location/remote availability) so I don't have to wait for a manual response to get basic information.
Acceptance criteria:

The automated confirmation email includes a short FAQ block or "While you wait" section covering 3–5 common questions
Questions are relevant to the service selected in the dropdown
Links to relevant pages (Services, Reports) included in the email body


10. Footer contact
US-19 — As a visitor on any page, I want to see contact details and a quick enquiry link in the footer so I never have to hunt for how to reach the firm.
Acceptance criteria:

Footer includes: firm email address (or mailto link), phone (if applicable), LinkedIn and any other social links, and a "Get in touch" button or link to the contact page
Footer is consistent across all pages
Footer also includes: copyright line, links to Privacy Policy (placeholder), and navigation links to all main sections


11. SEO & performance (non-functional)
US-20 — As the firm owner, I want every page to have correct meta titles, descriptions, and Open Graph tags so the site appears well in search results and link previews.
US-21 — As a visitor on a slow connection, I want the site to load within 3 seconds on the first visit so I don't bounce before seeing the content.
